# Fix plan 2026-09-07 — from the doc-vs-code audit

Derived from the game's OWN design docs (`MECHANICS.md`, `ECONOMY_DESIGN.md`,
`FIXLIST_2026-04-21.md`) checked against the code, not from any comparison with
another game. Every item below was verified by grepping for call sites and for
reads, not just writes: "dead" means the only callers are the defining `.cpp`
or the tests.

**Scope note.** `~/.claude/plans/i-would-like-you-rosy-diffie.md` is a
Civilization VI *comparison* document. It is NOT the spec for this game and
must not be treated as one. This game diverges deliberately — religion, for
one, is intended as a benefit/drawback axis (a better hold on large empires,
science friction in the modern age), which `MECHANICS.md` already calls the
"era-gated science-vs-devotion curve", not as a parallel belief economy.

**Working rules.**
- One item per commit. Each has its own acceptance criterion.
- Items marked **DECISION** must stop and ask. They are design calls, not
  implementation, and guessing them is how this plan goes wrong.
- Items marked **WIRE-OR-DELETE** have two acceptable outcomes. Deleting dead
  code is a real fix; leaving it looking implemented is not.
- No test-game runs (500-turn sims, golden re-blessing) without asking first.
- Watch for tests that pin an index, a count, or a constant into a content
  table. Three broke that way on 2026-09-07 for reasons unrelated to what they
  tested. Assert the relationship, not the number.

## Phase A — live bugs

Small, independent, and each one currently produces a wrong result in a shipped
game. Do these first; none needs a design call.

### A1. Two stockpile reads use the wrong good id
`src/simulation/city/Happiness.cpp:133` reads good **75** behind a comment
saying "Electronics (ID 75)". `ResourceTypes.hpp:251` defines
`SEMICONDUCTORS = 75`; `ELECTRONICS = 101`. The Electronics amenity bonus has
never fired.

`src/simulation/turn/TurnProcessor.cpp:671` reads good **77** commented
"Computers". `RUBBER_GOODS = 77`; `COMPUTERS_GOOD = 107`. Stockpiling rubber
grants the computer research bonus; computers grant nothing. The adjacent Glass
read (76) is correct by luck.

Fix both, and replace every magic-number stockpile read in `src/` with a
`goods::` constant — those three are the only ones, so the sweep is small.

*Acceptance:* a test asserting the Electronics amenity bonus and the Computers
science bonus each fire for their own good and not for a neighbour's id. Grep
proves no bare integer remains in a `getAmount` call in `src/`.

### A2. Nuclear Plant and Fusion Reactor pay fuel twice a turn
Charged once via `BuildingDef.ongoingFuel` in `consumeBuildingFuel`
(`EconomySimulation.cpp:384`) and again via `PowerPlantDef.fuelPerTurn` in
`computeCityPower` (`PowerGrid.cpp:74`). Buildings 29 and 35 are the only two
that set an ongoing fuel good at all.

Pick one owner of plant fuel. The power grid is the path that actually affects
output, so it is the better owner; the `ongoingFuel` column on those two rows
then goes to none.

*Acceptance:* a test that a fuelled Nuclear Plant consumes its uranium exactly
once per turn.

### A3. AI-vs-AI reparations bypass the deal system and can go negative
`AIDiplomacyController.cpp:110-127` transfers 10% of the loser's treasury with
no term record and no persistence, and `loser->addGold(-reparations)` is
unguarded. The deal path defends against exactly this at
`DealTerms.cpp:434-437`.

Minimum: clamp so a treasury cannot go negative. Better: route it through the
deal system, which makes it visible, saved, and available to a human. See B3,
which is the same seam.

*Acceptance:* a test that an AI with a near-empty treasury cannot be driven
below zero by reparations.

### A4. Robot-worker maintenance ticks twice per turn
`Automation.cpp:16-35` (via `TurnProcessor.cpp:1373`) and again inline at
`EconomySimulation.cpp:658-676`. Two writers of `automation.robotWorkers` per
turn. Delete one; the dedicated file is the better owner.

*Acceptance:* a test that robot upkeep is charged once per turn.

### A5. A spent builder survives on the validated request path
`requestPlaceImprovement` / `requestChop` / `requestHarvest`
(`BuilderActions.cpp:119,164,188`) and `requestRepair` (`UnitOrders.cpp:96`)
spend the charge but never remove the unit. Removal lives only in the UI and AI
wrappers, so the MCP and REST paths leave 0-charge builders on the map forever.

Move the consumption into the request layer, where every caller reaches it, and
drop it from the wrappers.

*Acceptance:* a test that a builder with one charge is gone after a
`requestPlaceImprovement`, driven through the request and not the UI.

### A6. Unsupplied units still heal
`SupplyLines.hpp:18` promises they cannot. The heal block
(`TurnProcessor.cpp:498-537`) never consults `supply()`.

*Acceptance:* a test that an unsupplied damaged unit does not heal and a
supplied one does.

## Phase B — dead code: wire it or delete it

Each of these looks implemented from the outside. Deleting is an acceptable and
sometimes better outcome; the unacceptable outcome is leaving it as is.

### B1. `pillageTrader` has no caller — trader cargo vanishes
`TradeRouteSystem.cpp:1487-1542`, declared and compiled, called by nothing.
`Combat.cpp:360-370` special-cases only the domestic Courier (type 32), so
killing an international Trader silently destroys its cargo and `carriedGold`.
Wire it into the combat kill path.

*Acceptance:* a test that killing a laden foreign trader transfers or drops its
cargo rather than voiding it.

### B2. Monopolies have no pricing power  **DECISION**
`GlobalMonopolyComponent::buyerPriceMultiplier` (`MonopolyPricing.hpp:62-71`)
has zero callers. A monopoly is a flat trickle of at most 100 gold per turn per
good and changes nothing about what buyers pay.

Wiring it into `Market.cpp` price formation is the obvious move, but it changes
the trade economy's shape, so confirm the intent before building it.

### B3. `DealTermType::WarReparations` is never constructed  **DECISION**
Enforcement (`DealTerms.cpp:424-447`), AI valuation, UI text and save
round-trip all exist. No surface builds the term: REST/MCP offers only
GoldLump / OpenBorders / NonAggression, the UI composer only GoldLump, the AI
only those plus CedeCity / CedeTile. The switch case cannot be entered.

Either expose it in the peace composer and the AI's peace valuation, or delete
the term and its four consumers. Pairs with A3.

### B4. Trade routes dangle when a city changes hands
No route-cancellation function exists repo-wide. `trader.destOwner` goes stale
while the arrival path reads the live owner, so the previous owner's trader
keeps walking and unloads into the conqueror's stockpile at the at-war rate.

*Acceptance:* a test that a route to a captured city is cancelled or retargeted,
and that its trader no longer pays the conqueror.

### B5. Alliance level 2/3 payloads are never read  **DECISION**
`ALLIANCE_TYPE_DEFS.level2Bonus` / `level3Bonus` and
`AllianceYieldModifiers::goldMult` / `combatBonus` have no read sites. A generic
+5% per level replaced the designed per-type effects (shared visibility, a free
eureka, shared Great Work slots, +5 combat near allies).

Implement the designed payloads or delete them and document that alliance
levels are a flat yield bump.

### B6. Building levels are frozen at 1
`CityBuildingLevelsComponent::upgrade()` / `setLevel()` have no caller outside
the header and tests, so `CAPACITY_TABLE` columns 2-3 and all of
`UPGRADE_COST_TABLE` are unreachable. Either give cities a way to upgrade a
building, or delete the unreachable columns.

### B7. Dead IndustrialRevolution bonuses
Zero callers outside the header: `cumulativeScienceMultiplier`,
`cumulativeTradeMultiplier`, `hasHighways`, `hasAutomation`, `hasCleanEnergy`,
and `revolutionPollutionMultiplier`. Robot slots bypass `hasAutomation()` and
test `currentRevolution >= Third` inline. Wire the ones that belong and delete
the rest.

### B8. Unit transport does not exist
`UnitComponent::cargoCapacity` / `cargo` are written at init and never read; the
serializer hardcodes 0 with a comment saying so; there is no load or unload
function. The game uses self-embarkation instead. Delete the fields, or decide
transports are wanted.  **DECISION** if the latter.

### B9. `BarbarianClans` is entirely dead
`GameState::m_barbarianClans` is never populated. `bribeClan` / `hireClan` have
no callers. `convertClanToCityState` is named in a header comment and has **no
definition anywhere**. Clan types, `isBribed`, `hiredBy` and `strength` are
never read. Only `barbarianSpawnUnit` and `encampmentDestroyReward` from that
file are live. Implement clans or delete the module.  **DECISION**

### B10. Two genetic-algorithm genome slots are inert
`integrationThreshold` and `integrationTurnsRequired` (slots 7 and 8) are packed
and unpacked but no simulation code reads them, so Integration-victory tuning is
a no-op and mutations on those bits are wasted. Wire them into the Integration
victory check or remove them from the genome.

### B11. Theological combat exists twice — my error, 2026-09-07
`src/simulation/religion/TheologicalCombat.cpp` (167 lines,
`resolveTheologicalCombat` + `purgeReligion`) already existed and was dead. A
second implementation was then added in `Religion.cpp`
(`requestTheologicalCombat`, commit `b066ef4`) and wired into `requestAttack`.
Both exist; only the new one runs.

Reconcile to one. Note the older file also contains the Inquisitor purge path,
which the new one lacks.  **DECISION**, because it is bound up with what
religion becomes — see D2.

## Phase C — content and coverage gaps

### C1. Around 70 of 78 unit types have no working upgrade path
`UnitTypeDef::upgradesTo` / `upgradeCost` on all 78 rows are dead: their only
readers, `Unit::upgradeTarget/canUpgrade/upgradeCost`
(`include/aoc/game/Unit.hpp:211-219`), have zero callers. The live path is an
8-row `UPGRADE_PATHS` table (`UnitUpgrade.cpp:31-38`), and the two disagree —
the unit table sends a Slinger to Archer, `UPGRADE_PATHS` to Crossbowman.

Make the unit table the single source of truth, delete `UPGRADE_PATHS`, and
give every unit with a successor a working path.

*Acceptance:* a table test that every unit whose `upgradesTo` is set can
actually be upgraded, and that no two tables describe the same upgrade.

### C2. Adjacency food is computed and read by nobody
`AdjacencyBonus::food` is summed by `cityAdjacencyYields`
(`DistrictAdjacency.cpp:209`) and has no consumer, while its five siblings all
reach city output. Feed it into city food the way the others are fed.

*Acceptance:* a test that a district adjacency granting food raises the city's
food yield.

### C3. Wonders are missing three things the docs describe
No national-versus-global distinction — `GlobalWonderTracker` applies
one-per-game to all 24, and `WonderDef` has no flag. No resource
prerequisites — `WonderLockReason::NoResource` exists only as a UI string that
no code produces. No Great Work slots — `greatWorkCapacity`
(`GreatWorks.cpp:20-30`) sums district buildings only and never consults
`city.wonders()`.

Three separate commits.

### C4. Barbarians never attack cities
`findNearestTarget` (`BarbarianController.cpp:63-83`) scans `player->units()`
only. No `resolveAttackOnCity` or `pressIntoCity` appears anywhere in the file.
They also never target city-states.

*Acceptance:* a test that a barbarian party adjacent to a defended city attacks
it. Note city HP and the siege path already exist and are the right seam.

### C5. Goody hut rewards that do nothing
The map-reveal reward is a log line with no fog-of-war call
(`GoodyHuts.cpp:157-162`), so a 10-in-100 roll gives literally nothing. The
documented culture reward grants 80 gold instead (`:135`). The eureka reward
fires only boosts keyed to building a wonder.

### C6. The era-difference combat modifier does not exist
`MECHANICS.md` describes it. `UnitEra` is read nowhere in `Combat.cpp` or
`CombatExtensions.cpp`; era only affects maintenance and food. Either implement
it or correct the doc — see Phase E.  **DECISION**

### C7. Ranged combat omits three modifier families
Civ ability `combatStrengthBonus`, government bonuses and
`greatPersonAuraBonus` are applied in the melee branch only
(`Combat.cpp:715-766`). The ranged branch already got formation, promotion,
flanking and fortification parity; this is the remainder.

### C8. City-states and barbarians respect zone of control but exert none
`ZoneOfControl.cpp:19` iterates `gameState.players()`, which by contract
excludes both. Silent asymmetry.

### C9. The 108 named great people have decorative abilities
Effects come from the 30-entry `GreatPersonDef` via `defId`; `namedId` drives
only names, Great Work attribution and UI. So Monet's "+200 tourism" is text.
Either give named people their own effects or drop the ability text.  **DECISION**

### C10. The economy has almost no test coverage
`tests/test_economy_invariants.cpp` covers bonds and one civic loyalty boost.
Nothing tests goods sinks, tool efficiency, fuel, power, monopoly, the
industrial revolution, science funding, pillage yield or capture transfer — and
Phase A found three live bugs in exactly that surface.

Add coverage as each Phase A and B item lands rather than as one large commit.

## Phase D — design calls, for the user

Do not implement these from a guess.

### D1. Consuming goods is strictly worse than hoarding them
The population drains (`EconomySimulation.cpp:575-579` and `1176-1191`) destroy
consumer goods with no reward for consuming and no penalty for lacking. Only
wheat's shortfall is read; the other three are computed and discarded, and one
drain's own comment says no downstream effect reads the result. Meanwhile the
rewards for merely holding goods saturate almost immediately: gold capped at 15
per city, amenities square-root scaled and capped at 4x base, soft cap 80 units.
Software (price 200) and Microchips (160) contribute zero passive income,
because the goods tax counts only four goods.

The marginal unit of a finished good is worth nothing. Fixing that is a design
decision about what the goods economy is FOR, not a bug fix.

### D2. What religion becomes
The intent stated on 2026-09-07: everyone can have religion; it gives a better
hold on large empires and causes science friction in the modern age. Half exists
already — `religionScienceCoefficient` is the era-gated science penalty. The
other half, religion helping hold a large empire, does not: nothing connects
religion to loyalty.

Also decide the fate of the Civ-VI-shaped religion work shipped on 2026-09-07
(`33de468`, `b066ef4`, `723a8b9`: belief effects, a 40-entry belief table, holy
cities, pressure decay, theological combat, doctrine by leader score). Keep as
extra depth, revert, or fold the useful parts into the loyalty side.

### D3. Embargoes are symmetric
`setEmbargo` sets both directions and there is no inbound/outbound split, so
World Congress sanctions create N fake-symmetric embargoes. FIXLIST H1.8 asked
for the split. Still open, and it is a model change rather than a bug.

### D4. Content table sizes
Current counts: 24 wonders, 51 buildings, 8 districts, 78 unit types, 76
recipes. If any should grow, that is a content decision with a golden re-bless
attached, and worth batching.

## Phase E — correct the docs

`MECHANICS.md` misleads a reader in both directions, which matters because it
is the closest thing to a spec.

- Unit count "~100+" is 78. The `UnitClass` list invents Builder, Spy and
  Diplomat (all three are `Civilian`) and omits AntiCavalry, Helicopter,
  Settler, Scout, Religious, Trader and Logistics.
- Tourism is described as "currently stubbed — header only"; it is implemented.
- Walls are listed as three tiers; there are four since 2026-09-06.
- Theological combat is pointed at `religion/TheologicalCombat.cpp`, which has
  no caller. Resolve alongside B11.
- `MECHANICS_ANALYSIS.md:11,93` says zone of control is missing; it is live.
- The 2026-04 barbarian null-pointer claim is stale; the pointer is set in all
  three hosts.
- `ECONOMY_DESIGN.md` says research is free, tool dependency is missing, and 46
  recipes. All three are wrong: funding is charged per turn, tools are modelled
  at 60% output, and there are 76 recipes.
